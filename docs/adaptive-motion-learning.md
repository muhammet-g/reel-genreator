# Adaptive motion learning for stronger first drafts

Creator direction, 2026-10-04: use the Nirox report, corrected mistakes, components
and sound assets to improve initial planning, visual execution and motion. Apply
experience with judgment; style examples remain adaptable to each new brief.

## Discover relevant experience

Start with the current project's brief and identity, then the learning-log index.
Select the few lessons relevant to its material, purpose and weakest dimensions.
The local [digital UI collection](../projects/style-references/digital-ui/README.md)
holds the adopted Nirox report, component snapshots, actual sound assets and evidence.
The [paper collection](../projects/style-references/paper/README.md) serves a different
material family. These collections belong to ignored projects/; when missing from
a checkout, state the gap and use available evidence rather than claim assets exist.

Names such as Nirox or Good Dragon select supplied identity data. Subject and
material determine which behavior fits: a paper fold may work in a new brand;
an electronic glitch may suit an interface or be inappropriate for that scene.
With no named identity, author a coherent palette/type/material system for the brief.

## Make experience change the initial plan

In the project's plan, record a short **experience applied** note:

- Current purpose and desired rhythm: showcase, explanation, story or another goal.
- Relevant prior mistake and its diagnosis; acceptance status and evidence.
- A concrete first-draft choice influenced by that experience.
- The mechanism/parameters adapted to this subject and identity.
- The frames, short sequence or listening feedback that will test the choice.

This can be a paragraph or the local authoring card. It is filled by the assistant
using the brief; routine decisions do not require a new creator questionnaire.
Alongside this note, use the [Remotion skill workflow](remotion-tools.md#skill-workflow-for-every-remotion-task)
to record which relevant skill/reference changed an implementation choice and what
evidence will test it. In the learning log, distinguish a recommendation read from
a result observed and a result accepted by the creator.
Choose an observable improvement, such as a meaningful carrier between scenes,
cleaner velocity at a handoff, stronger component anatomy or better sound material.
The amount of novelty follows the brief; a retained strength may be the right choice.

## Selection questions learned from actual corrections

| Experience | Initial design question | Adaptation and review |
|---|---|---|
| Unmotivated frame sway | What relationship does viewpoint motion reveal? | Use a stable, directed or expressive viewpoint according to meaning; inspect reading and interaction |
| Reference displaced brand | Which information is identity, which is choreography? | Retain actual brand data; adapt the reference mechanism |
| Generic shells before detail | What anatomy makes the object recognizable? | Design proportions, typography, material and meaningful content before polishing movement |
| Speed change mistaken for rhythm | What happens at takeoff, peak travel, braking and hold? | Author local responses and action spacing; adjust to showcase versus explanation |
| Collisions only visible mid-transition | Who occupies the focal area during the handoff? | Inspect layer order, outgoing/incoming content and pointer/nav duplication |
| Group exit replaced per-object action | Which part of the requested transformation belongs to each object? | Choose group or individual behavior from the brief; stagger when it explains relationships |
| Curved cards lacked separation/backs | How do spacing, perspective and rear material behave during deformation? | Derive geometry, inspect projected gaps and construct visible surfaces as needed |
| Release produced a velocity jump | Which position/direction/velocity is inherited at transfer? | Inspect adjoining frames; match response where continuity serves the action |
| Misaligned logo and jumping writing | Where is visible ink, and how is text width reserved? | Align actual rendered content and design reveal/layout together |
| Motor-like/paper-like digital effects | Does timbre fit the material, and do repeated tails merge? | Select sound by action/material; inspect overlap/peaks and obtain listening feedback |
| Particle distribution formed lines | Is procedural variation visually convincing? | Use deterministic variation and inspect actual images, not just runtime errors |
| Render could omit custom world | Does the chosen composition contain all authored layers and sound? | Verify the entry, assets and decoded output against meaningful reference frames |

These are diagnostic prompts. Their answers can legitimately include expressive
camera motion, a longer reading hold, a fold, an impact, a fade or silence.
Nirox's rejected paper timbre is a contextual mismatch, not a ban on paper Foley.
An engine sound can be appropriate for a vehicle; the failed UI mix is not a reason
to treat every tonal effect as an error. Exact Nirox dimensions, times and palette
remain case parameters, not universal thresholds.

## Sound as an adaptable material reference

Consult sound entries by action: contact, disruption, travel, transformation or
settling. Each entry preserves original bytes, decoded PCM, hash, source/license,
peak timing, prior usage and scoped feedback. Preview an appropriate candidate in
the new scene and choose onset, peak alignment, trim, gain, pan and tail from its
actual gesture. Use the project's existing audio workflow and immutable masters.

The creator liked V15 whooshes in their Nirox use. Sharper V16 clicks and the final
impact are implemented/measured examples awaiting specific listening acceptance.
Descriptions, waveforms and zero clipping do not demonstrate perceptual approval.
Related layers can share one accent; sound is not required for every moving property.
Music, silence and identity-specific timbres follow the current authorization/brief.

## Close the learning cycle

Review the first draft at designed states, transition frames/segments and final output.
Record which initial choice improved, what failed, how it was repaired and what the
creator actually accepted. Carry supported diagnoses forward; keep uncertain options
as candidates and supersede contradicted claims. Adding a reference is not itself
proof that future visual performance has improved.

A project-local component is an example. Extract a reusable API when recurrence
and its parameters justify it; record testing and limits separately from visual
acceptance. These files provide persistent project guidance, not model retraining
or unattended background learning.
